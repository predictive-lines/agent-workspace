## 1. Purpose & Philosophy

Excel Fire Protection is a union fire sprinkler contractor operating in Michigan's Upper Peninsula. We compete for skilled tradespeople against shops in metro areas that offer lower cost of living and milder winters. This plan exists to close that gap — not with a line item on a benefits summary, but with a direct financial stake in the work our people do every day.

The intent is simple: when the company makes money, the people who did the work share in it. The structure is designed so that every crew member can draw a straight line between finishing a job well and money in their pocket.

This plan supplements — and does not replace or modify — any benefits, wages, or terms established under the applicable Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) with UA Local 669.

---

## 2. Plan Overview

The plan has two components:

| Component | Source | Trigger | Timing |
|-----------|--------|---------|--------|
| **Project Profit Share** | 10% of project-level gross profit | Retainage release | Within 30 days of collection |
| **Annual Service Pool** | 5% of company-level net profit | Fiscal year close | Paid within 90 days of FYE |

**Project Profit Share** is the behavioral driver. It creates a per-job incentive tied to project completion and quality — paid when the retainage check clears, so the company never pays out before it collects.

**Annual Service Pool** covers service, inspection, and repair work that doesn't carry retainage, and serves as a top-up that rewards overall company performance. It also ensures T&M and small-job employees participate meaningfully.

## 3. Eligibility

### 3.1 Eligible Employees
All full-time field employees of Excel Fire Protection, including:
- Foremen
- Journeymen (UA Local 669)
- Apprentices (all class levels)

### 3.2 Exclusions
- Officers and owners (Justin Miller, Jaclyn Miller)
- Administrative/office staff (covered under a separate incentive program, if applicable)
- Subcontractors and temporary labor
- Employees terminated for cause prior to distribution date

### 3.3 Minimum Tenure
- **Project Profit Share:** Employee must have worked on the project to receive a share from that project. No minimum tenure required — if you were on the job, you're in the pool.
- **Annual Service Pool:** Must be employed for a minimum of 90 calendar days during the fiscal year and be actively employed on the distribution date.

### 3.4 Pro-Rata Participation
Employees who join or depart mid-year (voluntarily and in good standing) receive a pro-rata share of the Annual Service Pool based on months of eligible employment ÷ 12.

## 4. Share Allocation — Weighting System

Distributions are not split equally. They're allocated using a **point-based weighting system** that reflects role, responsibility, and tenure.

### 4.1 Base Share Weights

| Role | Weight | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Foreman | **1.75×** | Runs the jobs. His efficiency directly drives profitability. Carries scheduling, coordination, and quality accountability. |
| Journeyman | **1.00×** (base unit) | Skilled tradesperson. The standard production unit. |
| Apprentice | **Scaled by tenure** (see below) | Growing into the role. Incentive increases as contribution increases. |

### 4.2 Apprentice Tenure Scale

| Apprentice Year | Weight | Approximate Class Level |
|-----------------|--------|------------------------|
| Year 1 | 0.25× | Class 1–2 |
| Year 2 | 0.40× | Class 3–4 |
| Year 3 | 0.55× | Class 5–6 |
| Year 4 | 0.70× | Class 7–8 |
| Year 5 | 0.85× | Class 9–10 |
| Journeyman (post-turnout) | 1.00× | — |

Tenure is measured from date of hire with Excel Fire Protection, not union registration date. An apprentice who joins at Class 5 from another shop starts at Year 1 weight and progresses from there — loyalty to *this* company is what's being rewarded.

### 4.3 Allocation Formula

Each eligible employee's share of a given pool:

```
Employee Payout = Pool Amount × (Employee Weight ÷ Sum of All Eligible Weights)
```

**Example:** A project generates $30,000 gross profit. The 10% Project Profit Share pool = $3,000. Crew on the project: 1 Foreman (1.75×), 2 Journeymen (1.00× each), 1 Year-2 Apprentice (0.40×).

| Role | Weight | Share | Payout |
|------|--------|-------|--------|
| Foreman | 1.75 | 42.2% | $1,265 |
| Journeyman A | 1.00 | 24.1% | $723 |
| Journeyman B | 1.00 | 24.1% | $723 |
| Apprentice | 0.40 | 9.6% | $289 |
| **Total** | **4.15** | **100%** | **$3,000** |

## 5. Project Profit Share — Detailed Mechanics

### 5.1 Eligible Projects
All installation and construction projects with a retainage clause in the contract. This includes:
- New construction
- Tenant improvement / retrofit
- System additions and modifications

### 5.2 Project Gross Profit Calculation

```
Project Gross Profit = Total Contract Revenue (including change orders)
                     − Direct Materials (pipe, fittings, heads, hangers, etc.)
                     − Direct Labor Cost (wages + union benefits + payroll taxes)
                     − Subcontractor Costs (if any)
                     − Equipment Rental directly attributed to the project
```

**Not deducted:** Overhead allocation, officer salaries, insurance, rent, vehicle fleet, or any company-level expense. This is *gross* profit at the project level — the measure of how well the crew executed the work.

### 5.3 Profit Share Pool

```
Project Profit Share Pool = Project Gross Profit × 10%
```

### 5.4 Eligible Crew
Only employees who physically worked on the project are eligible for that project's share. Hours worked on the project (tracked via timesheets/job costing) determine eligibility — there is no minimum hour threshold, but the allocation is weighted by hours contributed.

**Adjusted formula when hours vary:**

```
Employee Payout = Pool × (Employee Weight × Employee Hours on Project)
                        ÷ Σ(All Eligible Weights × Hours)
```

This ensures a JM who worked 400 hours on a project gets a proportionally larger share than one who worked 40 hours, while still applying the role-based weight multiplier.

### 5.5 Payout Trigger & Timing
- Payout is triggered when **retainage is released and collected** by the company.
- Payment to eligible employees occurs within **30 calendar days** of retainage collection.
- If retainage is released in installments, the profit share is paid proportionally with each installment.

### 5.6 Warranty / Callback Clawback
If a warranty claim or callback on a completed project results in material cost to the company (labor + materials exceeding $500), the cost is deducted from the originating project's profit share pool *before* distribution.

- If retainage hasn't been collected yet: the pool is reduced before payout.
- If the profit share has already been paid: the cost is deducted from the responsible crew members' next available Project Profit Share payout(s). No cash clawback from previously paid amounts.

This creates accountability — a clean punch list and quality work protects the bonus.

## 6. Annual Service Pool — Detailed Mechanics

### 6.1 Purpose
Covers all revenue-generating activity that doesn't fall under the Project Profit Share:
- Fire sprinkler inspections
- Service calls and repairs
- T&M (time and materials) work
- Any project without a retainage clause

Also serves as a company-wide performance bonus that rewards collective success.

### 6.2 Net Profit Calculation

```
Company Net Profit = Fiscal Year Net Income (per company financials)
                   − Officer Compensation & Distributions
                   − One-Time / Non-Recurring Items (at management discretion)
```

This is bottom-line net income with owner comp backed out, so the pool reflects operational performance — not how much the owners chose to pay themselves.

### 6.3 Service Pool

```
Annual Service Pool = Adjusted Net Profit × 5%
```

### 6.4 Allocation
All eligible employees (see Section 3) share the Annual Service Pool using the same point-based weighting system (Section 4). Unlike the Project Profit Share, hours worked on specific jobs don't factor in — this is a company-wide pool allocated by role weight and pro-rated tenure.

### 6.5 Payout Timing
- Calculated after the fiscal year financial statements are finalized.
- Paid within **90 calendar days** of fiscal year end.
- Must be actively employed on the date of distribution (exception: pro-rata for good-standing departures per Section 3.4).

## 7. Profitability Thresholds & Guardrails

### 7.1 Minimum Profit Threshold — Project Profit Share
No Project Profit Share is paid on any project where:
- Project gross margin is **less than 20%**, OR
- Project gross profit is **less than $5,000**

Low-margin or loss-making projects don't generate a bonus. This prevents perverse incentives where rushing a job to completion creates a payout on a project that actually hurt the company.

### 7.2 Minimum Profit Threshold — Annual Service Pool
No Annual Service Pool is distributed if:
- Company adjusted net profit is **negative**, OR
- Company net profit margin is **less than 5%**

In lean years, the company needs to retain every dollar. The team should understand: a rising tide lifts all boats, and a falling tide means everyone tightens up.

### 7.3 Pool Cap
The combined total of all Project Profit Shares and the Annual Service Pool in any fiscal year shall not exceed **15% of company adjusted net profit**. If the sum of project-level payouts approaches this cap, the Annual Service Pool is reduced first (to zero if necessary), and then project-level payouts are scaled proportionally.

This protects the company from a scenario where high project-level margins create outsized payouts in a year where the company-level financials don't support it.

### 7.4 Discretionary Adjustment
Management reserves the right to adjust the plan percentage, pool size, or payout timing in any given year based on:
- Extraordinary expenses or losses
- Cash flow constraints
- Capital investment requirements
- Changes in debt service obligations

Any adjustment will be communicated to employees in writing at least 30 days before the affected distribution date. The plan is a **voluntary employer benefit**, not an entitlement or guaranteed compensation.

## 8. Service / Repair Work — Price Adjustment

For service calls, inspections, and repair work that do not carry a retainage line item:

- These jobs are covered **exclusively** by the Annual Service Pool (Section 6).
- To ensure pricing accounts for the profit share obligation, all service/repair quotes should include a **10% markup above standard pricing** to explicitly fund the bonus pool and maintain target margins.
- This markup is an internal pricing guideline — not communicated to customers as a "profit sharing surcharge."

This keeps the model clean: install/construction work funds its own bonuses through retainage timing; service work funds its bonuses through pricing that accounts for the cost.

## 9. Communication & Transparency

### 9.1 Project-Level Visibility
After each project is completed and the profit share is calculated, the Foreman and crew will receive a **Project Profit Share Statement** showing:
- Total contract value and change orders
- Direct costs (materials, labor, subs)
- Gross profit and gross margin %
- Profit share pool amount
- Individual payout and how it was calculated

The crew should be able to see exactly how their work translated to their bonus. No black boxes.

### 9.2 Annual Reporting
Within 30 days of fiscal year end, all eligible employees will receive:
- Company financial summary (revenue, gross profit, net profit — not full financials)
- Annual Service Pool amount
- Individual allocation and payout amount
- Year-over-year comparison

### 9.3 Ongoing Education
At plan launch (and annually thereafter), a brief session will explain:
- How project profitability is calculated
- How individual actions affect the bonus (e.g., material waste, rework, schedule delays)
- Current-year financial targets

If people don't understand how their work connects to their payout, the incentive doesn't work.

## 10. Tax & Legal

### 10.1 Tax Treatment
All profit share payments are treated as **supplemental wages** subject to federal and state income tax withholding, FICA (Social Security and Medicare), and applicable state taxes. Payments are reported on the employee's W-2.

### 10.2 ERISA Considerations
This plan is designed as a **non-qualified, discretionary bonus program** — not an ERISA-qualified profit sharing or retirement plan. It does not involve employee contributions, deferred compensation, or trust accounts. All payments are current-year cash compensation.

Consult with the company's employment attorney and CPA to confirm ERISA exemption and proper tax treatment before the first distribution.

### 10.3 Union Compliance
This plan is a supplemental, employer-funded benefit that operates independently of the CBA with UA Local 669. It does not modify or replace any negotiated wages, benefits, or working conditions.

Review with labor counsel before implementation to confirm the plan does not constitute a mandatory subject of bargaining or conflict with any CBA provision.

## Appendix B: Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Fiscal Year** | October 1 through September 30 |
| **Retainage** | A percentage of contract value (typically 10%) withheld by the customer until project completion and final inspection |
| **Gross Profit (Project)** | Contract revenue minus direct project costs (materials, labor, subs, equipment) |
| **Net Profit (Company)** | Bottom-line net income per company financials, adjusted per Section 6.2 |
| **Turnout** | Completion of the UA apprenticeship program and advancement to Journeyman status |
| **Good Standing** | Employee departure that is voluntary (resignation with notice) or due to layoff — not termination for cause |
